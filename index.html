02 · Research

Understand people first. Then build to learn.

My MHCI training gives me the methods and my engineering background lets me test ideas quickly. I like research that ends in something you can put in front of people, whether that's a prototype, a playtest, or a decision.

01

Discover

Find out what people are actually trying to do and where the friction is.

Interviews · contextual inquiry · think-alouds · diary studies
02

Synthesize

Turn messy observations into patterns, models, and clear problem statements.

Affinity mapping · journey maps · personas · flow models
03

Prototype

Build the cheapest thing that can answer the question, from paper to working code.

Storyboards · speed dating · Figma · working prototypes
04

Evaluate

Measure whether it works for real people, then decide what to change.

Usability testing · playtesting · surveys · heuristic evaluation

We generated ten interaction concepts and ranked them by how cheaply each one could be proven. We mapped what a browser extension can and can't do before committing, and designed a "proposal you approve" pattern so that nothing changes your work without consent.

"Can a light, retro platformer carry a serious story about doping in sport?"

We used action and enigma codes to plan how the gameplay itself tells the story. We tuned difficulty to escalate across the levels and built debug tooling (hitboxes, live stats, level skip) so playtests stay fast.

Pass the Mic

Social design

"How do you keep a group conversation fair when everyone is racing to reply?"

We designed turn-taking as a social mechanic. Claims expire after ten minutes, and you can't claim a reply to your own clip, so two friends can't ping-pong the whole conversation and quieter people get a turn.

UCREIxDPersuasive DesignDesign of AI Products

Principle 01

Prototype the question, not the product.

The first build should answer one thing. At Nova we ranked ten ideas by how cheaply each could be proven, then built the one we could test by Sunday.

Principle 02

Rules and constants are design decisions.

A ten-minute claim window or a pitch-smoothing value shapes how something feels as much as any screen. They deserve testing with real people, not just a guess.

Principle 03

Make testing cheap enough to do often.

Debug overlays, level skips, and fake data are research tools. The easier a playtest is to run, the more of them actually happen.
