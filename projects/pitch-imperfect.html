Overview

Karaoke that actually listens

Most casual karaoke games score you on whether you made noise at the right time. We wanted one that cares whether you're in tune, runs in a browser with no install, and is still fun when you're bad at it. The name says it all.

You pick a song (Twinkle Twinkle Little Star or Fireflies), choose solo or duo mode, and sing along with the track. Lyrics advance line by line, a pitch bar shows your note against the target, and your score builds as you go.

How it works

From air pressure to a score

  1. Capture. getUserMedia opens the mic and feeds a Web Audio AnalyserNode, which hands us 2,048 raw samples every animation frame.
  2. Detect. The pitchy library runs the McLeod Pitch Method on those samples and returns a frequency plus a clarity score. We ignore anything below 0.7 clarity or outside 50 to 1000 Hz, which filters out breaths, room noise, and consonants.
  3. Smooth. Raw pitch jitters, so we keep a running average that weights new readings at 10%. That keeps the needle readable without lagging too far behind.
  4. Score. Each lyric line has a target frequency. Every voiced frame compares your pitch to the target: within 8% earns 15 points, within 18% earns 8, and within 30% earns 4. The maximum possible score grows with every frame you sing, so accuracy is fair however long you hold a note.
// The scoring tiers from the hackathon app const diffRatio = Math.abs(freq - line.targetFreq) / line.targetFreq; let delta = diffRatio < 0.08 ? 15 : diffRatio < 0.18 ? 8 : diffRatio < 0.30 ? 4 : 0; setMaxScore(prev => prev + 15); setScore(prev => prev + delta);
Design decisions

Forgiving where it matters

  • Tiered scoring instead of pass/fail. Being close still earns something. For a party game, encouragement keeps people singing.
  • Per-line targets. Scoring every individual note would punish casual singers. One target per line rewards getting into the right range.
  • Silence isn't failure. Frames without a clear pitch aren't counted against you, so taking a breath doesn't hurt your grade.
  • Any-octave mode (added for this page). The original targets sit around G4, which is high for many voices. Folding your pitch into the target's octave lets anyone play.
Reflection

What I learned

Real-time audio is unforgiving. Everything has to fit inside a single animation frame, and the difference between "feels responsive" and "feels broken" was a few smoothing constants. If I took it further, I'd run a quick playtest on smoothing strength and scoring thresholds. Those are UX decisions disguised as DSP parameters, and they deserve real users rather than our guesses.
